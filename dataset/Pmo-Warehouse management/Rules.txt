1. A reorder is triggered automatically when the on-hand stock of an item falls below its predefined reorder threshold.
2. The reorder quantity is calculated based on the item's average daily demand and its supplier lead time.
3. Supplier selection for each reorder considers cost, delivery time, and the supplier's historical reliability rating.
4. Once an order is placed, the inventory system must be updated with the expected delivery dates immediately.
5. Incoming stock is inspected for quality before it is accepted, registered, and placed on shelves.
6. Received goods that fail quality inspection are quarantined and returned to the supplier within 5 working days.
7. Inventory levels are updated in the system as soon as the restock is completed.
8. If the expected delivery date passes without delivery, an escalation is sent to the procurement team within 2 working days.
9. Items with a shelf life shorter than 90 days must be stored on a first-expiry-first-out basis.
10. No stock may be received without a matching, open purchase order in the system.
11. Physical stock counts are reconciled with the system records at least once per quarter.
12. Items in negative stock are blocked from sale until the inventory records are corrected.
13. Restocking of high-volume items is performed during night shifts to avoid disruption of daily operations.
14. If a supplier's reliability rating drops below 70 out of 100, reorders are automatically diverted to the backup supplier.
15. The warehouse manager must approve any reorder exceeding $50,000 in total value.
