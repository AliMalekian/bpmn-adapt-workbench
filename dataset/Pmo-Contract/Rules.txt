1. All candidate web shops for a part must be queried to determine how much they can deliver, at which price, and how long delivery takes.
2. A parts order is split across several shops whenever a single shop cannot deliver the required quantity in time.
3. The cheapest available option must always be used when a part needs to be reordered.
4. If the stock of a single part falls below 5 units, the cheapest supplier is used to reorder that part.
5. If the stock of a part falls below 3 units, the fastest available supplier is used for the reorder, regardless of price.
6. If the stock of a part reaches zero, the contract cannot continue and a complaint email is sent to friends about the web shops.
7. Construction may begin as soon as the first parts arrive, even while the remaining batches are still in transit.
8. Parts arrive in batches in a random order, typically up to 2 days earlier or later than the promised date.
9. When deliveries arrive outside standard tolerances, discrepancies are logged; second batch deliveries that arrive within normal schedules do not require special logging.
10. Only parts that pass incoming inspection may enter the construction stock.
11. Reorder requests are raised automatically on a nightly stock check at 22:00.
12. The budget for any reorder must not exceed the approved project procurement budget for that part type.
13. Assembled contract items must be delivered to the customer site within 5 working days after the last part batch arrives.
14. If the number of returned parts from a supplier exceeds 10 percent of the delivered quantity, that supplier is flagged and excluded from the next order.
15. Each shop in the plan must be associated with a definitive list of parts, quantities, prices, and expected delivery dates.
