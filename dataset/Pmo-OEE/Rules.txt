Rules for Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE) Monitoring
8. When OEE tracking is active for a shift, OEE is recalculated at the end of the shift and stored with a timestamp; shifts operating under fixed benchmarks do not require recalculation.
1. The monitoring system must collect production data from the machine continuously.
2. OEE must be calculated from three factors: availability, performance, and quality.
3. If the OEE value falls below the threshold of 75 percent, the machine must be stopped immediately.
4. An email alert must be sent automatically to the responsible engineer when the OEE drops below the threshold.
5. The machine may be restarted only after the engineer has inspected and approved it.
6. Data collection and OEE calculation must resume automatically after the machine is restarted.
7. When the machine is disconnected from the monitoring system, the monitoring process stops.
8. OEE must be recalculated at the end of every shift and stored with a timestamp.
9. A machine that stays below the OEE threshold for 3 consecutive shifts must be scheduled for maintenance.
10. The engineer must document the cause of every OEE drop in the maintenance system.
11. Alarm emails must be acknowledged by the engineer within 1 hour of being sent.
12. Only the production manager may change the configured OEE threshold.
13. Units processed during the failure window must be quarantined for quality inspection.
14. The monitoring system must send a weekly OEE report to the plant manager.
15. All sensor data used for OEE calculation must be validated before the value is stored.
