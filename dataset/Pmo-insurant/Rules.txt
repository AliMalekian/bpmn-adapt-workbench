1. If insurance can launch a potential subrogation claim against an insurant, the case officer must first record the details of the possibility before proceeding.
2. A subrogation payment request may be sent to an insurant only if the case officer confirms that the possibility of recourse is genuinely valid.
3. The case officer must set a reminder for payment follow-up on the day the payment request is issued.
4. If recourse is not possible, the case must be closed within 10 business days without issuing a payment request.
5. The case is closed only after the received money is booked to the correct claim account.
6. An insurant who disagrees with the recourse must raise the objection within 14 days of receiving the payment request.
7. If the insurant's reasoning for disagreement is found to be correct, the case is closed without further collection action.
8. If the insurant's reasoning is found to be incorrect, the case is forwarded to a collection agency within 5 business days.
9. If the disagreement deadline is reached and no payment has been received, the case is automatically forwarded to the collection agency.
10. The collection agency may add a collection fee of up to 15% of the claim amount to the outstanding balance.
11. Payment requests above $5,000 must be reviewed and approved by a senior claims officer before being sent.
12. A claim without payment and without active action for more than 180 days is flagged for write-off review.
13. All communication with the insurant must be documented in the claim file before the case is closed.
14. Cases with a claim value below $200 may be waived if the estimated collection cost exceeds the claim value.
15. If a subrogated payment is received but booked to the wrong account, the booking must be corrected within 3 business days.
